Monsoon cloudbursts fill a street in minutes. Most warnings say only that it will rain.

A campus the size of KIET floods one low corner at a time: the lane behind a hostel, the underpass by the main gate, the inlet that has been silting up since June. City-scale warnings never get down to that level.

Drishti models the campus on a 5-metre grid, lets rain run over the real terrain and into a modelled drain network, and forecasts depth in every street cell for the next three hours. Every number on screen says whether it was observed, derived or modelled.

How it works

One loop, every five minutes.

  1. 01

    Read the sky

    Radar mosaic and eight rain gauges are pulled in and checked for spikes, frozen sensors, clock drift and gaps. Gauges correct the radar's bias.

  2. 02

    Nowcast the rain

    Optical flow tracks the storm; a 20-member STEPS ensemble extrapolates it for three hours and blends toward weather-model guidance after the first hour.

  3. 03

    Update the twin

    A coupled model routes water over the terrain and through the drains — inlets, pipes, a pump sump, outfalls — conserving mass to one part in a hundred million.

  4. 04

    Forecast the flood

    A U-Net trained on 290 physics runs turns every rain member into a depth map in a fraction of a second, so uncertainty is carried all the way to the street.

  5. 05

    Look closer where it matters

    Hotspots — high risk, high spread, or next to a building entrance — get a full physics re-run, then roads, routes and building access are scored.

These scores come from a synthetic twin experiment: a hidden storm and drain state stand in for reality. They show the system works end to end, not how it would score against a real flood. The ensemble is still too confident — its P10–P90 band misses the truth more often than it should — and that is the next thing to fix.

Specification

What sits underneath.

TerrainCopernicus GLO-30 surface model, resampled to 5 m · Tier 1 data
DomainKIET campus boundary (OSM), 76 × 104 cells, buildings and roads burned in
Drainage63 nodes, 61 links, pump sump, 2 outfalls — synthetic, clearly marked unverified
Rain1 km radar mosaic over 192 × 192 km, 8 gauges, weather-model blending past +60 min
Forecast0–180 min at 14 lead times, 20 members, P10 / P50 / P90 and exceedance at 10, 20, 30 cm
PhysicsLocal-inertial 1D pipes, diffusive-wave 2D surface, weir/orifice inlets, Horton or SCS-CN infiltration
Degraded modesRadar lost → gauges only, uncertainty ×1.5, forecast flagged; faulty sensors rejected
